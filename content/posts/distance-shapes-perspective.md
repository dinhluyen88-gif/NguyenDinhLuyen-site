I have walked quite a few trekking routes.

Each one leaves a different feeling. Some overwhelm you with scenery, some stay with you because they were hard, and some are simply one more milestone on the journey.

But Tateyama was the first trek that sent me home with more thoughts than photographs.

What I brought back was not a conquered summit. It was a point of view.

---

Seen from afar, the range looks like a perfect painting.

Deep green forest. Clear lakes. Bands of white ice. Peak after majestic peak.

That is the view anyone can see.

Once you step inside, the painting fades. In its place come long climbs, rough rock, muddy stretches, streams you have to find a way across, and steps you have to place with care.

That was when I realised: the mountain had not changed at all. Only my distance from the mountain had changed.

---

## Companies are the same

From the outside, we usually see the best of them.

A big brand. A successful product. A workplace people dream of. A close-knit team. A business that keeps growing.

Step inside, and the picture is no longer only green.

Behind every result is a long list of problems to solve. Behind every decision are pressure and trade-offs. Behind every success are many rounds of trying and fixing, failing and starting again.

The interesting part is that the company has not changed either. Only our distance from it has.

---

## Nobody sees the whole mountain

Then I noticed one more thing.

No two people in a company see the same picture. Not because one of them is more right, but because each is standing somewhere different.

- Outsiders see results.
- Insiders see the journey.
- Newcomers see opportunity.
- Veterans see the challenges.
- Managers see people.
- Leaders see responsibility.

Nobody sees the whole mountain. Each of us sees only the part our position allows.

Perhaps that is why we argue so easily, disagree so easily, even judge one another so easily. Not because anyone is stubborn, but because each of us is speaking from the part of the picture we can see.

---

What I learned on this trip was not how to climb. It was how to look at things with more empathy.

The more I experience, the more I believe this:

> From far enough away, we admire.
> From close enough, we judge.
> Only by walking alongside do we understand.

Understand that behind a beautiful picture there are always many people quietly getting through stretches of rock, mud and slope.

Maybe that is the real beauty of a company. Not the picture everyone sees, but the people who are making that picture together.
